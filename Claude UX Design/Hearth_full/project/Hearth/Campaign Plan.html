The region

The Salt Marches

A low, drowned coast of tide-flats and failing fishing towns, ruled in name by a distant crown and in fact by whoever holds the Gate. The sea gives less each year, and the people have learned to ask fewer questions of anyone who promises more.

Mood: quiet dread. Salt, fog, and the sense that something patient is owed.

The factions

Who holds what

The Gate guard keep an order that benefits the Gate. The quarter-folk protect each other quietly and trust no authority. A shrine to a forgotten harbor-god still draws the desperate.

Reveal — you haven't learned this
The truth of it

What the Hollow Man is

He is not taking the children for himself. He is paying a tithe — and the thing he pays was here long before the Gate, sleeping under the salt, and the town's forgetting is the first symptom of its waking.

The stakes

If nothing is done

The disappearances accelerate with the tides. The Gate's denial is not cowardice but complicity — and the longer it holds, the more the marsh remembers what it is owed.

Where you are

Saltcross · the poor quarter

A knot of leaning streets around a dry fountain and a faceless shrine. Home to the net-mender and the families who've lost the most. The old fishmarket sits at its edge — shuttered, and a poor place to be caught after dark.

Seen in passing

Thornveil Gate

The fortified customs-house that gives the region its real government. Clean, lamplit, and indifferent. The Gate Captain's word is law within sight of its walls.

The quarry

The Hollow Man

Once a Gate clerk named Edran Voss, who made a bargain to save his own daughter and has been paying for it with other people's ever since. Not evil so much as emptied. He can be reasoned with — at a terrible price.

Met · obstacle

Captain Mire

The Gate's authority in Saltcross. Calls the disappearances "runaways" and means to keep it that way. Whether from fear, bribery, or worse is not yet clear.

wary
Lore fragment

The harbor-god worn faceless

Every quarter has its shrine, and every shrine its drowned little god. The folk leave salt and bread and do not name it, because the oldest rule of the marsh is that a thing remembered grows hungry. The faceless shrine in the square is the oldest of them.

Theme

The cost of forgetting

This campaign turns on a single idea: that looking away has a price, and someone always pays it. Every authority in the Marches has chosen not to see — and the marsh keeps the ledger.
